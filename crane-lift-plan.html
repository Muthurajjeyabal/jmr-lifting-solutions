01 — What's inside a JMR crane lift plan

Twelve pages. Every calculation shown.

Not a checklist. Not a template with your name pasted in. A real engineering document that stands up to third-party review, insurance audits, and client sign-off.

01

Cover & sign-off block

Plan number, revision, project reference, Appointed Person signature, LEEA certification number, applicable standards. The audit trail begins on page one.

02

Load data & centre of gravity

Weight verified against vendor drawings, weighing certificates, or FEA models where required. Centre of gravity plotted, lifting point loads calculated per attachment.

03

Crane selection & configuration

Boom length, jib, counterweight, outrigger extension. Reach diagram overlaid on site plan. Alternatives considered where the primary crane is marginal.

04

Load chart utilisation

Working radius, gross load, capacity from chart, utilisation percentage with Dynamic Amplification Factor applied. Utilisation capped per BS 7121 and client procedures — with margin shown.

05

Rigging configuration

Sling type, WLL, angle, safety factor. Shackles sized per API/ASME. Spreader beams engineered where sling angles would exceed limits. Every component certified.

06

Sling & rigging load calculations

Vertical component, horizontal component, tension per leg. Design factor of safety 5:1 (slings) / 4:1 (shackles) verified. Load path traced from hook to load.

07

Ground bearing pressure

Outrigger reaction calculated for the worst-case radius and orientation. Bearing pressure checked against ground capacity. Crane mats or piling specified where the ground would fail.

08

Risk assessment (BS 7121 format)

Every hazard scored Likelihood × Severity, controls listed, residual risk scored again. High residuals trigger design changes — not warning stickers.

09

Method statement

Step-by-step sequence: pre-lift checks, trial lift, transit path, set-down. Hold points, communication protocol, weather thresholds, exclusion zones. What good looks like at every stage.

10

Roles & responsibilities

Appointed Person, Lift Supervisor, crane operator, banksman, riggers. Who is authorised to stop the lift. Who signs the pre-lift check.

11

Approvals & references

Client sign-off block, third-party review block, standards referenced, drawings referenced. The plan is the closing document for the operation.

+

Delivered as PDF and native

Signed PDF for the file, native source for future revisions. Revision control on every issue.

02 — When you need a crane lift plan

If it isn't routine, it needs a plan.

Non-routine industrial lifts

Refinery reactors, columns, exchangers, transformers, gearboxes, generators. Anything where the crane isn't doing the exact same lift it did yesterday.

Tandem & dual-crane lifts

Two cranes on one load require a dedicated lift plan with load-share calculations, tilt limits, and synchronised control procedures. Non-negotiable.

Blind lifts & obstructed sight lines

Operator cannot see the load or landing point. Requires banksman protocol, communication procedure, and rehearsed sequence documented in the plan.

Lifts near live plant or personnel

Near energised electrical, live pipelines, occupied buildings, or public areas. Exclusion zones and emergency-stop procedures must be engineered.

Offshore & marine lifts

Rig-to-vessel, vessel-to-vessel, splash zone. DNV-ST-N001 governs. Dynamic amplification, weather windows, and vessel motion must be modelled.

Utilisation above 75%

Any lift approaching crane rated capacity is a critical lift by BS 7121 definition. Full engineered plan is mandatory — no exceptions.

05 — Standards we engineer against

Every plan referenced. Every clause traceable.

BS 7121
Safe use of cranes

Part 1 (general), Part 3 (mobile cranes), Part 5 (tower cranes), Part 11 (offshore). The primary reference framework for every lift plan we produce.

LOLER 1998
Lifting Operations & Lifting Equipment Regulations

UK statutory instrument governing planning, competent-person requirements, and thorough examination of lifting equipment.

ASME B30
Cranes & rigging

B30.5 (mobile cranes), B30.9 (slings), B30.10 (hooks), B30.26 (rigging hardware). The North American reference set — cross-checked where dual-code compliance is required.

DNV-ST-N001
Marine operations

Governing standard for offshore lifts, marine operations, weather-restricted operations, and lifted-object-in-water design cases.

API RP 2D
Offshore crane operations

American Petroleum Institute recommended practice for the operation and maintenance of offshore cranes.

LEEA COPSULE
Code of Practice for Safe Use of Lifting Equipment

Lifting Equipment Engineers Association guidance — the reference our Appointed Person is certified against.

06 — Frequently asked

Crane lift plan questions we hear most.

What is a crane lift plan?

A crane lift plan is the engineering document that authorises a lifting operation. It specifies the load, the crane, the rigging, the ground conditions, the exclusion zones, the sequence of operations, the personnel roles, and the residual risks. Under BS 7121 and LOLER 1998 it must be prepared by a competent person before any non-routine lift.

When is a crane lift plan legally required?

LOLER 1998 (UK) and BS 7121 (international) require a documented lift plan for any lifting operation that is not routine and repetitive. In practice this covers essentially all industrial, construction, and offshore lifts. The plan must be prepared by a competent Appointed Person and reviewed before the lift proceeds.

What does a JMR crane lift plan contain?

Twelve pages: cover with sign-off, scope, load data with centre of gravity, crane selection, load chart utilisation with DAF, rigging configuration, sling and rigging load calculations, ground bearing pressure, BS 7121 risk assessment (L×S), method statement, roles and responsibilities, and approvals with references.

If a project needs an on-site Lifting Supervisor or a second Appointed Person, we engage vetted certified professionals from our trusted network on a project-specific basis.

How long does a lift plan take?

Standard mobile crane lift plans in 3–5 working days from receipt of load data and site information. Complex tandem lifts, offshore lifts, and critical lifts requiring finite element checks typically take 7–14 days. Emergency turnaround is available on request.
